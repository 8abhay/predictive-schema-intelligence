# Publication-readiness checklist

**Status of venue research: NOT DONE.** No journal, conference or workshop has been verified in this session, so no venue is recommended here. Do not rely on any venue until you verify the items below from the venue's *official* pages.

## Before choosing a venue (your tasks, ~1 day)
- [ ] Complete the literature search (data contracts, lineage/impact analysis, observability, change-level defect prediction) and re-run the novelty audit.
- [ ] Open every cited paper; read it (not just the abstract); fill the TODOs in `references.bib` and the manuscript.
- [ ] Cite DuckDB and scikit-learn after verifying their reference metadata.
- [ ] Publishing independently (no institution): confirm each venue accepts independent researchers and does not require an institutional affiliation, institutional e-mail or endorsement letter. Create an ORCID iD (free) if the venue asks for one.

## Venue vetting (for each candidate)
- [ ] Scope fits (data management / software engineering / data engineering / applied ML) and accepts short or proof-of-concept papers.
- [ ] Peer-review process described on the official site; editorial board is real and verifiable.
- [ ] Author guidelines and template downloaded; page limit checked.
- [ ] Article-processing charge: confirm "no mandatory fee" on the official page; read waiver policy.
- [ ] Indexing claims verified at the indexer itself (e.g., Scopus/DBLP/DOAJ/Web of Science), not on the venue's own page.
- [ ] Preprint and prior-publication policy allows arXiv/preprint posting.
- [ ] Warning signs: guaranteed acceptance, "publication in days", vague review, fee requested before review, unverifiable indexing.

## Manuscript
- [ ] Re-run `python src/run_all.py` on your Windows machine and confirm numbers match.
- [ ] Rewrite in your own words where needed; add your name/affiliation; add acknowledgements/AI-assistance disclosure per venue policy.
- [ ] Convert to the venue template (LaTeX or Word); check figures (PNG at 200 dpi) and table widths.
- [ ] Add licence file (e.g., MIT for code); archive the repository (e.g., Zenodo) if you want a DOI.
- [ ] Cover letter (state: synthetic proof-of-concept; limitations).

## Needs YOUR explicit approval (I will not do these)
Submitting to any journal/conference, accepting publication terms, paying any fee, posting to a preprint server, or agreeing to copyright/licence transfer.

## Realism check
Peer review typically takes weeks to months. Within four days the achievable milestones are: verified literature matrix, reproducible experiments, a complete draft, and a submission-ready package. Acceptance, indexing or publication within four days cannot be promised.

## Preprint vs journal vs peer-reviewed
*Submission* = sending for review. *Peer-reviewed publication* = published after review and acceptance. *Preprint* = public copy; not peer-reviewed by itself.
